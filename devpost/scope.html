Hushmate Less shush, more hush.

A live web app where housemates share their quiet times and can nudge each other anonymously when someone's being loud.

The unique kernel

Anonymous nudging

When someone is being loud, you can nudge them without it coming from you. The reminder is phrased as if the app itself is speaking, never as a message from a housemate, so it stays anonymous for everyone, even in a two-person house.

  • Housemates join the house together. In your words, “housemates agreeing to be on the platform means they acknowledge boundaries.” A nudge is a gentle reminder, not a confrontation.
  • Disturbance is judged by the person trying to rest, not by a meter: there's no hardware and no always-on microphone.

How it fits together

Who it's for

Students in shared housing first. More broadly, anyone sharing a place with housemates, which is common in Vancouver, where renting is more common than owning.

The picture: B is trying to sleep while A is loud in the living room. Today, B says nothing.

“I had nothing because I was an introvert.”

Some students also move between places, like weekends with friends or family. They need the house they're currently in to be the one that counts.

Why it matters to you

It comes from your own experience living with “louder than average housemates” as an introvert who said nothing. You think “most students who live in dorms can relate”, and you see it as “pretty novel and unique in this space”.

Your goal for this build: a first iteration that reaches 80% or more of what you have in mind, with no features you aren't aware of.

The core loop

Once

Set weekly quiet times

They can differ by day, for example one schedule for Monday, Wednesday and Friday and another for the rest of the week.

Day to day

Live alongside each other

  • See who's resting now
  • Give a heads-up before you're likely to make noise: “coming home late, might make late dinner”
When disturbed

Nudge, anonymously

Someone being loud gets an anonymous nudge from you, in the app's voice, at any time, not only during your own quiet time. They can reply “got it”, also anonymously.

Why people return: nudges come to them as notifications, and the house's quiet streak gives positive reinforcement.

What “working” looks like

A live, hosted app. Open the link and use it straight away, with nothing to install. Onboarding shows how to turn on browser notifications. On iPhone, notifications need the app added to the Home Screen, and onboarding teaches that.

The demo

  1. Open the link: no install. Onboarding turns on notifications (and teaches “add to Home Screen” on iPhone).
  2. Join the demo group, preloaded with housemates, weekly quiet-time schedules (some in the morning too) and a historical streak.
  3. See who's resting now, then nudge one of the preloaded housemates.
  4. With no real housemate around, the demo group simulates one: a “TEST” nudge arrives within 10 minutes of finishing onboarding.
  5. Reply “got it”.
  6. More simulated nudges follow at random intervals of 30 minutes to 2 hours.
The proving moment: a gentle, anonymous nudge arrives as a real notification on the judge's own device, from a house they joined minutes ago.

Inspiration & identity

  • The app is the messenger. Nudges read as a gentle reminder from the app (third person), never as a complaint from a person.
  • Simple, well executed. “Something simple that can be well executed.”
  • Positive reinforcement over enforcement. A house streak, not penalties.
  • Not a chores-and-bills app. Housemate apps like Flatastic have no noise or quiet-time features. This one focuses only on quiet time.
  • Name and identity (decided 2026-10-03): Hushmate, “Less shush, more hush.” A “cozy midnight” look: dark pastels, flat, no mascot, Fredoka and Nunito, dark and light mode. Details are in prd.md › Look and Feel.
